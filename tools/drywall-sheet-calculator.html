How Many Sheets of Drywall Do I Need?

Divide your total wall (and ceiling, if applicable) area by the coverage of one sheet, then add a waste allowance for cuts around doors, windows, and outlets. A standard 4×8 sheet covers 32 square feet; larger 4×12 sheets reduce the number of seams needed on tall walls.

Standard Drywall Sheet Sizes

Sheet SizeCoverageBest For
4×8 ft32 ft²Standard rooms, easiest to handle
4×10 ft40 ft²Fewer horizontal seams on tall walls
4×12 ft48 ft²Large open walls, garages

Don't forget joint compound, tape, and screws — as a rule of thumb, budget roughly 1 box of screws and 1 gallon of joint compound per 500-1000 square feet of drywall.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many square feet does a sheet of drywall cover?

A standard 4x8 sheet covers 32 square feet; 4x10 covers 40 sq ft, and 4x12 covers 48 sq ft.

How much waste allowance should I add for drywall?

10% is standard for rooms with typical door and window openings; more complex rooms may need 15%.

How much joint compound do I need?

Roughly 1 gallon of joint compound per 500-1000 square feet of drywall, depending on the number of coats.
