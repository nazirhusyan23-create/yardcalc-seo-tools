Concrete Driveway Cost in 2026: Full Price Breakdown

A concrete driveway typically costs between $6 and $12 per square foot installed, depending on your region, driveway thickness, and finish. Here's exactly what drives that price up or down, plus real cost estimates for common driveway sizes.

8 min read

Average Cost Per Square Foot

Most homeowners pay $6-12 per square foot for a standard broom-finished concrete driveway, including materials and labor. Decorative finishes like stamped or stained concrete push that range to $12-20 per square foot.

What Affects Your Final Price

Beyond square footage, several factors shift your quote significantly:

  • Thickness: Driveways for cars need 4-6 inches versus 3-4 inches for walkways, directly increasing material volume and cost.
  • Site prep: Removing an old driveway, grading uneven ground, or adding a gravel sub-base all add labor cost before the pour even begins.
  • Reinforcement: Rebar or wire mesh adds $0.50-2 per square foot but prevents cracking under vehicle weight.
  • Region: Labor and delivery costs vary significantly by city — always get 2-3 local quotes before committing.
  • Access: Difficult truck access for concrete delivery can add short-load or pump fees.

Concrete vs. Gravel: Which Costs Less Long-Term?

Gravel driveways cost roughly $1-3 per square foot installed — a fraction of concrete's upfront price. However, gravel needs periodic regrading and top-ups every few years, while a well-poured concrete driveway can last 25-30 years with minimal maintenance. For long-term value, concrete usually wins if you're staying in the home more than 7-10 years; for a quick, budget fix, gravel is hard to beat. Frequently Asked Questions

Is a gravel or concrete driveway cheaper?

Gravel is cheaper upfront, roughly one-third the cost of concrete, but needs periodic top-ups and regrading, while concrete lasts decades with minimal upkeep.

How long does a concrete driveway last?

A properly installed concrete driveway typically lasts 25-30 years with basic sealing maintenance every few years.

Can I pour a concrete driveway myself?

Small driveways under 200 square feet are DIY-feasible for experienced homeowners, but larger pours usually need a crew to finish the surface before the concrete sets.
